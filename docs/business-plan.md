**A 12-month plan to take A&A Flooring from a Coquitlam owner-op shop to a top-3 search result for "flooring contractor" across the Lower Mainland.**

## 1. Executive summary

**Where we are.** A small, owner-operated shop in Coquitlam since 2016. Strong craftsmanship, weak digital presence. New website (this repo) ships the SEO foundation; everything below is what to do next.

**Where we're going.** Top-3 organic ranking for "flooring contractor [Coquitlam | Port Moody | Burnaby | Vancouver]" within 12 months. 5–8 quote requests per week from digital channels. Recognized as the *best stair-and-trim shop* in the Tri-Cities.

**How we get there.** Don't fight Woodpecker (40 years) or BC Floors (4.5★, full GVA coverage) head-on. Pick three sharp wedges — **(1) custom millwork & stairs**, **(2) Tri-Cities local-first positioning**, **(3) the photography-and-reviews flywheel** — and run them harder than anyone else.

**The investment.** Year-1 marketing spend of **$18,000–$32,000 CAD** ($1,500–$2,700/mo). Below the line is the website (sunk cost). Above the line: Google Ads, GBP investment, a HomeStars/Houzz subscription, and a part-time content/social hand.

## 3. The market — Greater Vancouver Area

### Pricing benchmarks (2026, installed)

| Service | Low | Typical | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vinyl plank (LVP) | $5/sqft | $7/sqft | $10+/sqft |
| Laminate | $5/sqft | $6.50/sqft | $9/sqft |
| Engineered hardwood | $9/sqft | $12/sqft | $16/sqft |
| Solid hardwood | $12/sqft | $15/sqft | $20+/sqft |
| Hardwood replacement (incl. demo) | $14/sqft | $20/sqft | $30+/sqft |
| Stair tread (per tread) | $80 | $150 | $300 |
| Minimum job fee | $400 | $500 | $600 |

(Sources: VC Floor, Silver Touch Renovation, Sandover Floors, Floors Depot, HomeStars Cost Guide.)

### Customer segments to target (pick the top 2; ignore the rest)

1. **Tri-Cities homeowners doing one-room or whole-floor refresh** — $5K–$25K per job. Highest volume. **Primary target.**
2. **Burnaby/Vancouver condo owners replacing carpet with LVP** — $4K–$12K per job. Strata-savvy is the differentiator. **Secondary target.**
3. New build / spec home volume — fights against contracted-in installers. *Skip.*
4. Commercial / TI — long sales cycle, paperwork-heavy. *Skip until year 2.*
5. Insurance / restoration — requires WCB clearance + insurance company onboarding. *Skip until year 2.*

### Demand seasonality (BC)

- **Mar–Jun**: peak. Spring renos, pre-summer-listings.
- **Sep–Nov**: secondary peak. Pre-holiday "we want it done before the in-laws arrive."
- **Dec–Feb**: slow. Use for content production, photography, training.
- **Jul–Aug**: medium. Vacation season slows decisions.

Spend **70% of the marketing budget Mar–Jun and Sep–Nov.** Don't burn ad dollars in mid-summer or late December.

## 5. Differentiation & brand strategy

Pick three things and beat the drum on every page, every ad, every post.

### Three pillars

**Pillar 1 — "The stair shop."**  
Custom stair treads, risers, casings, and matching trim, milled in our Coquitlam shop. No one else owns this. It's defensible, it's photogenic, it commands premium pricing, and it lifts the whole brand into "craftsman" territory. Every hardwood project ends at a stair anyway — own the hardest part.

**Pillar 2 — "Tri-Cities first."**  
We are the local shop. Same-day site visits in Coquitlam, Port Moody, Port Coquitlam. We know your strata. We've worked your block. Use first-name references to neighborhoods (Burquitlam, Maillardville, Westwood Plateau, Inlet Centre) on the website, in ads, and in Google Business posts.

**Pillar 3 — "One shop, one crew."**  
We don't subcontract. We don't use day labor. The same person who quotes the job is on-site day one. This is huge for trust. Every quote and every email should mention it.

### Naming clarity

There is an "AAA Flooring" in Vancouver (30+ years). Customers will confuse the names. Solutions:
- Drop the "Accessories" from the everyday brand → just **A&A Flooring** in marketing copy. Reserve the legal "A&A Flooring Accessories" for the footer / legal lines.
- Use a recognizable logomark consistently. Right now `/images/logo-color.png` and `logo-black.png` are the assets — invest **$300–800** in a clean wordmark refresh from a freelancer (99designs, Fiverr Pro, or a local designer).
- File the trade name `A&A Flooring` with the BC business registry if not already done.

### Visual signature

The current website is the right direction (warm cream bg, walnut accent, Fraunces serif). Lock this in. Use it everywhere — Instagram template, GBP cover, ad creative, business card, truck wrap. Consistency creates recall. Make a one-page brand sheet so every vendor uses the same colors and fonts.

## 7. Marketing channels — what to actually do

Each section: **what it is, what to spend, what to expect, the one-thing trap.**

### 7.1 Google Business Profile (GBP) — the highest-leverage hour you can spend

**Spend:** $0/mo cash. ~3 hours/month time.

**What it is.** The map listing that shows up when someone searches "flooring near me." Outranks the website for local intent.

**Do this:**
- Already covered in `docs/gbp-and-cutover.md`. The 80/20: complete every field, post weekly, upload 2–3 photos weekly, respond to every review within 48h.
- **The one-thing trap:** people set it up, then never post. GBP weights *recent activity* heavily. A profile with weekly posts and photos beats one with 3× more reviews but no recent activity.

**KPI:** From "Search → A&A in top-3 of Maps Pack for 'flooring contractor coquitlam'" within 6 months.

### 7.2 Local SEO + content (organic)

**Spend:** $0–500/mo if writing in-house; $300–600 per blog post if hiring a writer.

**What it is.** The website and blog ranking organically. Long compound returns — slow start, big payoff at month 6+.

**Do this:**
- Six blog posts in year one. Topics ranked by ROI:
  1. "How much does hardwood flooring cost in Vancouver?" (high search volume, money keyword)
  2. "Strata flooring rules in Burnaby/Coquitlam: what's required" (long-tail, high intent)
  3. "Solid vs engineered hardwood — which is right for a BC home?" (educational, top-of-funnel)
  4. "Custom stair treads: a guide for Lower Mainland renos" (owns our pillar)
  5. "Refinishing vs replacing hardwood — when each makes sense" (rescue-existing-floor segment)
  6. "Best flooring for a Vancouver basement" (LVP/waterproof segment)
- Each post 1,200–1,800 words, with 2+ photos, internal links to relevant service/city pages, and a quote-form CTA at the bottom.
- **The one-thing trap:** writing about features instead of customer questions. Always write the post answering an actual question a customer asked you on a job.

**KPI:** Organic clicks/month ≥ 800 by month 12.

### 7.3 Google Local Services Ads (LSA) — the cheat code

**Spend:** Pay-per-lead, ~$25–80/lead in flooring (Lower Mainland).

**What it is.** Google's "Google Guarantee" green-checkmark ads at the top of search. They show up *above* regular Google Ads and the map pack. Only verified local businesses qualify. **This is the single highest-ROI channel for home services in 2026.**

**Do this:**
- Apply at https://ads.google.com/local-services-ads/. Requires:
  - Business license (BC business registration)
  - Liability insurance certificate
  - WCB / WorkSafeBC clearance letter
  - Background check (for the owner)
- Once approved, leads come in via the LSA dashboard. You only pay for leads you accept (calls + messages).
- **The one-thing trap:** ignoring the inbox. LSA penalizes slow responses by lowering ranking. Aim < 30 minute response on every LSA call/message during business hours.

**KPI:** 8–15 LSA leads/month by month 6, < $80/lead.

### 7.4 Google Search Ads — for keywords LSA doesn't cover

**Spend:** Start $500/mo, scale to $1,200–2,000/mo if profitable.

**What it is.** Traditional pay-per-click for queries with clear commercial intent.

**Do this:**
- Skip "flooring" alone (CPC $8–15, broad). Target verticals:
  - "hardwood stairs vancouver" (~$3 CPC, low competition, our pillar)
  - "custom stair treads coquitlam" (~$2 CPC, near-zero competition)
  - "engineered hardwood port moody" (~$4 CPC, hyper-local)
  - "vinyl plank installation burnaby" (~$5 CPC)
  - "strata flooring contractor burnaby" (~$3 CPC, intent-rich)
- Send each keyword cluster to its matching service-city page on the site (e.g. "hardwood stairs coquitlam" → `/stair-treads-casings-coquitlam`). Don't dump everything on the home page.
- Use **Phrase match** primarily. **Negative keywords**: "diy", "free", "carpet", "tile", "linoleum", "sale", "tools", "blade".
- **The one-thing trap:** broad-match keywords. They burn budget on irrelevant searches.

**Industry benchmarks (home services, 2026):**

| Metric | Typical | Good | A&A target |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPC (search, contractors) | $5–12 | < $4 | $3–6 |
| Conversion rate | 2.6–7% | 8%+ | 6% (we're niche/local) |
| Cost per lead | $90–170 | < $90 | < $120 |
| Cost per won job | $300–600 | < $300 | < $400 |

(Sources: LocaliQ 2025 Home Services Search Ad Benchmarks; Hatch Google Ads cost-benchmarks; BG Collective 2026 Remodeler Ads Statistics.)

**KPI:** $120 cost-per-lead by month 6.

### 7.5 HomeStars + Houzz — third-party trust

**Spend:** HomeStars paid tier $75–150/mo. Houzz free tier sufficient initially.

**What it is.** Vertical platforms where homeowners shop contractors. Trust signal. Older customers (45+) often start here.

**Do this:**
- Claim both. Upload 30+ project photos with detailed captions and tags.
- HomeStars paid tier unlocks lead-gen and "verified" badge — worth it once you have 10+ reviews on the platform.
- Reach out to past customers with a HomeStars-specific review request (they have a template flow).
- **The one-thing trap:** treating these as fire-and-forget. Same activity rule as GBP — fresh photos and replies matter.

**KPI:** 10+ HomeStars reviews by month 6. "Top Rated" or "Best of" badge by month 12.

### 7.6 Meta Ads (Instagram + Facebook) — the photogenic one

**Spend:** $300–600/mo, starting month 10.

**What it is.** Visually-driven retargeting and local awareness ads.

**Do this:**
- Don't run Meta Ads cold for direct lead-gen — flooring isn't an impulse buy.
- Run **two campaign types only:**
  1. *Retargeting* — anyone who visited aa-flooring.ca but didn't fill the form. Show them before/after stair photos for 14 days.
  2. *Local awareness* — radius around Coquitlam/Port Moody, age 30–60, homeowners. Showcase before/after Reels. Optimize for video views, not link clicks.
- **The one-thing trap:** running Meta Ads before you have great photos. Without good photos this channel doesn't work. Skip until you have month-3 photoshoot done.

**KPI:** Site retargeting CTR > 1.5%; brand recall in customer surveys (ad-hoc) by month 12.

### 7.7 Photography — the silent multiplier

**Spend:** $400–800 per shoot, twice a year.

**What it is.** Pro photos of finished work. Multiplies the effectiveness of every other channel.

**Do this:**
- Hire a real-estate / interior photographer (NOT a wedding photographer). They know how to shoot floors and rooms with proper lighting. Local listings on Lensa / Marketplace start ~$400 for a half-day.
- Two shoots per year, 4–6 finished projects per shoot.
- Always get a signed photo release from the homeowner before the shoot.
- For day-to-day: smartphone process photos are fine. iPhone 12+ in landscape mode, well-lit, captioned with city + service. No filters.
- **The one-thing trap:** thinking your phone photos look "good enough." They don't. Pro photos move the needle 3× on every channel.

**KPI:** 60+ pro-quality photos in rotation by month 6.

### 7.8 Strategic partnerships — the slow burn

**Spend:** Time, plus 5–10% referral fees.

**What it is.** Other professionals who repeatedly need a flooring contractor.

**Targets:**
- **Interior designers** in the Tri-Cities (start with the 5 that have the most active Houzz profiles in Coquitlam)
- **Real estate agents** specializing in Burquitlam / Inlet Centre / Port Moody — they need fast pre-listing refreshes ($3K–8K jobs, 2-week turnaround)
- **Strata management firms** (Crosby, Tribe, FirstService Residential) — owners ask them for contractor recommendations
- **Stager companies** — staging often reveals worn flooring; they refer
- **General contractors** who don't have an in-house flooring crew

**Do this:**
- Coffee, not pitch. First meeting is "tell me about how you work, here's how we work."
- 5% referral fee for any closed job from their referral.
- Custom one-pager you leave with them (PDF), branded, with photos.
- **The one-thing trap:** pitching too hard. Designers refer to people they trust, not people who ask. Earn it over 3–6 months.

**KPI:** 3 active partnerships sending 1+ qualified leads/month each by month 12.

### 7.9 Email — the asset everyone ignores

**Spend:** $0/mo until 1,000 contacts. Then ~$40/mo (ConvertKit / Buttondown).

**What it is.** Past customers and quote requesters. Repeat business + referrals.

**Do this:**
- Add every customer to a simple list (just an exported CSV is fine to start).
- One quarterly email: a project showcase, a tip, a referral ask. Keep it short.
- After every completed job, add to a "completed customers" segment and email at year 1, year 2, year 5 with maintenance tips and "still happy?" check-in.
- **The one-thing trap:** going pro tools too early. A Google Sheet + manual sends works fine until 500+ contacts.

**KPI:** 200+ contacts by month 6, 500+ by month 12.

## 8. Sales operations

Marketing brings leads. *Sales* turns them into jobs. This is where most contractors leak revenue.

### Response SLA

- **Phone calls during business hours:** answered live, or returned within 30 min. (Use a virtual assistant service for ~$200/mo if needed.)
- **Quote form submissions:** acknowledge within 1 hour, schedule a site visit within 24 hours.
- **GBP messages, LSA, HomeStars:** < 30 min during 9–6.

The single biggest predictor of close rate for home-services leads is **response time**. The contractor who calls back in 5 minutes wins ~70% of the time. The one who calls back in 24h wins ~10%.

### The site visit

- Always in person. Always.
- 30–45 min on site. Bring measuring tape, moisture meter, sample boards.
- Quote on the spot when possible (carry a tablet with the quote template). Same-day quotes close 2× more often than 3-day-later quotes.
- If quoting later, commit to a specific time ("by tomorrow noon") and meet it.

### The quote document

A branded PDF beats a text email every time. Sections:
1. Project summary (what we heard from them)
2. Scope (rooms, sqft, materials, prep, finishing)
3. Itemized cost (clear)
4. Timeline (start date, completion date)
5. What's included / not included
6. Warranty statement
7. Payment terms (e.g. 30% deposit, 50% midway, 20% on completion)

Add 3–4 thumbnail photos of similar finished work — relevant to *their* job. Customers buy with their eyes.

### Win/loss tracking

Simple spreadsheet, one row per quote:
- Date | Source | Customer | Service | Quote $ | Result (won/lost/pending) | Reason | Site visit Y/N | Hours-to-quote

Weekly: review last 5 lost quotes. Pattern? Pricing? Timeline? Communication? Adjust.

### Pricing positioning

Not the cheapest. Not the most expensive. **Top of the middle third.** Customers who pick the cheapest will fight you over every detail; customers who pick the most expensive expect concierge service. The middle-top customers want craft, communication, and on-time delivery — the things A&A is good at.
